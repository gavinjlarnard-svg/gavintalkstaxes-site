# GavinTalksTaxes Tax Organizer

Doing your own taxes isn't the hard part. Finding everything is.

Your W-2 shows up as an email from your payroll company. The bank's 1099 is a "your tax document is ready" link. The brokerage 1099 can run dozens of pages and sits behind a login. And the SSA-1099 is in a drawer somewhere.

Tax Organizer is a Chrome extension I built for people who file their own return. It does the gathering with you.

## What it does

- Reads last year's return (a PDF on your computer) and lists the forms you should expect this year.
- Searches your Gmail for W-2s, 1099s and other tax forms. Read-only. Emails that look like scams get flagged and are never imported.
- Reads the tax-documents page on your payroll site (ADP, Workday, Gusto and others) while you're signed in. It only reads. It never clicks or types.
- Shows what you have and what's still missing.
- Builds a filing guide with every box on every form and the line it goes on. When you've typed everything into your tax software, check a few totals against it. If one is off, you usually skipped a form or typed one twice.
- Flags possible deductions to check, each with the IRS page that explains it.

## Where your information goes

Nowhere. There's no server behind this and no sign-up. Everything stays on your own computer, encrypted with a passphrase only you know. I never see it. The extension has no ads and no analytics, and nothing is sold.

The details are in the [privacy policy](/tax-organizer/privacy/).

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't file your return, and it isn't tax advice. A flagged deduction means "check this," not "claim this." If your year has something harder in it (wash sales, crypto, Marketplace insurance, an early IRA withdrawal), it tells you, and points you to free IRS help. TCE offers free help to people 60 and older.
